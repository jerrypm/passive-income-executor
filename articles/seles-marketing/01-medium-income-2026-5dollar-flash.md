## Why I'm sharing this for free instead of behind the paywall

Here's the strategy I figured out the hard way:

> **For the first 60–90 days, you don't need money from Medium. You need followers, trust, and reads.**

Medium pays based on member read-time. To get member read-time, you need members to *find you*. To get found, you need to be **shareable, indexable, and free**.

The minute you slap "Member-only story" on a new account's article, you cut off 80% of your potential audience. Non-members hit the paywall and bounce. No social shares. No Google traffic. No Reddit citations. Your reach collapses to "people who already pay Medium and somehow stumbled on you."

That's a death loop for a new writer.

**My approach instead:**

1. **Months 1–2:** Publish 80% free articles. No paywall. Drive every social channel I have to those articles. Get the follower count past 100.
2. **Month 3+:** Start gating my *best* work behind the paywall. Free stuff still ships, but the "premium" pieces are now monetized.
3. **Month 6+:** Lean into the new February 17, 2026 referral mechanic — every non-member who signs up because of my article is a chunk of recurring income.

That's what got me from $0 (Sep 2024) → $16 (Oct) → $72 (Nov).

## The 4 things I wish I'd known on Day 1

If I could time-travel back to Sep 2024, I'd hand myself a sticky note with these:

### 1. Free articles aren't "leaving money on the table." They're an investment.
Every free article builds your follower count, your topic authority, and your search presence. Once you have 200+ followers and 3 articles ranking on Google, *then* paywall makes sense. Before that — you're paywalling an empty room.

### 2. Member read-time > pageviews. Always.
A 2,400-word post that 600 members finish pays more than a 900-word post that 4,000 non-members skim. Medium pays for *the reading*, not the *opening*. Write longer when the topic deserves it.

### 3. The Dec–Feb dip will happen.
Almost every Medium writer I've talked to has one. You'll have a good month, try to replicate it the obvious way, and fail. The dip is a signal that you optimized for the wrong variable. Use it to refactor your approach, not to quit.

### 4. The Feb 17, 2026 referral bonus is bigger than people realize.
When a non-member hits your paywall and signs up because of your story, you earn a chunk of that membership fee. **Free articles that link to a paywalled "deep dive"** are now the highest-ROI move on the platform. Almost nobody is doing this properly yet.
