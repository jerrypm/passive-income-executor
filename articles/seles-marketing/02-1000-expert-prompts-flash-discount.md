## The "prompt fatigue" problem nobody admits to

If you've used ChatGPT or Claude for more than 6 months, you've felt this. The honeymoon is over. The novelty wore off. You're using it for real work now — emails, code, plans, copy, research — and you're hitting a wall.

Not a wall of *capability*. The models are better than ever.

A wall of *vocabulary*. You don't have the words to ask for what you want.

That's why every serious AI user eventually does one of three things:

1. **Builds a personal prompt library** (slow, takes months)
2. **Memorizes 20 prompt frameworks** (also slow, mostly forgettable)
3. **Buys a curated pack from someone who already did the work** (smart, but the market is a swamp)

I went with option 3. The market is, indeed, a swamp.

## What's broken with most prompt packs

I bought five before I made my own. Here's the pattern I kept hitting:

**The "100,000 prompts!!" packs:** Quantity is a lie. 95% are duplicates, typos, or prompts that say *"Write a poem about love"*. Useless.

**The single-niche packs:** $30 for "200 real estate prompts." If you're not in real estate, dead weight. Even if you are, half the prompts assume US-only context.

**The "no framework" packs:** Just a list of fill-in-the-blank prompts. No reason behind the structure. You learn nothing. Next month you're back to vague prompts.

I wanted a pack that taught me *how to think about prompts* while giving me 1,000 ready-to-paste ones.

## The framework: Role + Goal + Constraint + Format

Every good prompt is the same shape. Once you see it, you can't unsee it.

- **Role**: "You are a senior SaaS copywriter who specializes in product launches."
- **Goal**: "Write a 6-email welcome sequence for a new B2B sign-up."
- **Constraint**: "Tone: warm but professional. Avoid hype words. Each email under 180 words."
- **Format**: "Output as: Email 1 - Subject - Body. Repeat for 2-6."

Compare that to "write a welcome email for my SaaS."

The output isn't *slightly* better. It's a different universe. The AI stops guessing what you want and starts executing.

Once you internalize that shape, *every* prompt you write gets sharper. Every prompt you read makes sense.
