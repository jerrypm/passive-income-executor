# 8 Article Skeletons

These are full markdown templates with `[FILL IN: ...]` placeholders, target word counts per section, and recommended hook patterns from Chapter 4. Open any skeleton in your text editor, save a copy with your working title, fill in every bracket, then paste into Medium — headings and code blocks transfer cleanly. Each skeleton targets the word count range that balances demonstrating expertise with completion rate.

---

## 1. Tech Listicle

Use when listing tools, libraries, or shortcuts in a technical niche — readers come for the list but stay for the depth on each item.

**Hook pattern:** Usage or Credibility
**Word counts:** Hook 100–150 · Each item 100–150 · Bonus item 100–150 · CTA 50–100

```markdown
# [N] [Items] I Use [Daily / Every Day / In Production]

[FILL IN — Hook: 3-5 sentences. Pain → promise → proof. No backstory opener.]

## Why This List Now

[FILL IN — 1 paragraph: why these items, why this moment in your workflow.]

## 1. [Item name]

[FILL IN — 100-150 words. What it is, why you use it, when it matters.]

## 2. [Item name]

[FILL IN — 100-150 words. Same structure.]

## 3. [Item name]

[FILL IN — 100-150 words. Same structure.]

[FILL IN — 100-150 words.]
```

**Word counts:** Hook 100–200 · Backstory 100–150 · Conflict 200–300 · Choice 200–300 · Lesson 200–300 · Reflection 100–200

```markdown

[FILL IN — 100-150 words: enough backstory for the conflict to make sense.]
```

## 4. Contrarian Opinion

Use when you have a genuine counter-position to a widely held belief and evidence to back it — thin evidence reads as hot take, not argument.

**Hook pattern:** Contrarian
**Word counts:** Hook 100–150 · Belief 100–150 · Why wrong 200–300 · Evidence 300–500 · Alternative 200–300 · Caveat 100–150

```markdown
# [N] Things Most [Audience] Get Wrong About [Topic]

[FILL IN — Hook: name the belief upfront. Don't bury the lede.]

## The Belief Most People Hold

[FILL IN — 100-150 words: steelman the conventional wisdom before you knock it down.]

## Why It's Wrong

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: one clear counter-claim, not a list of weak ones.]

## The Evidence

[FILL IN — 300-500 words: data, case studies, specific numbers. This earns the contrarian framing.]

## What To Do Instead

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: actionable alternative — not just "avoid X," give them Y.]

## The Caveat

[FILL IN — 100-150 words: when the conventional wisdom IS right. Builds trust, deflects rebuttals.]
```

## 5. Case Study

Use when a specific subject has a measurable before/after — lead with the transformation, not the methodology.

**Hook pattern:** Outcome
**Word counts:** Hook 100–150 · Subject 100–200 · Before 200–300 · What was done 400–600 · After 200–300 · Lessons 200–300

```markdown
# How [Subject] Went from [Before State] to [After State] in [Time Period]

[FILL IN — Hook: lead with the transformation. Numbers help.]

## The Subject

[FILL IN — 100-200 words: who or what, and why it's instructive for this audience.]

## The Before State

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: specific metrics — revenue, users, error rate. Quantify everything.]

## What Was Done

[FILL IN — 400-600 words: the playbook in order. Include decisions that didn't work alongside ones that did.]

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: same metrics as Before, with the delta. Show the math.]
```

## 8. Story

Use for pure narrative where the scene carries meaning and analysis stays minimal — unlike the Personal Essay, this skeleton foregrounds the scene over the lesson.

**Hook pattern:** Regret or Secret
**Word counts:** Hook 100–150 · Situation 200–300 · Conflict 300–500 · Choice 200–300 · Outcome 200–300 · Lesson 100–200

```markdown
# [Title — concrete and specific, not abstract]

[FILL IN — Hook: drop into a specific moment. First-person, present-tense if it fits the scene.]

## The Situation

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: where, when, with whom. Enough detail to make the conflict land.]

## The Conflict

[FILL IN — 300-500 words: the moment things got complicated. Specific dialogue, objects, numbers. Show it — don't summarize.]

## The Choice

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: what you or the subject decided. Show the internal stakes as clearly as the external action.]

## The Outcome

[FILL IN — 200-300 words: what happened next, in concrete terms. Don't soften the consequences.]

## The Lesson

[FILL IN — 100-200 words: what the story means now. One clear takeaway — the story earns one.]
```
